## Context

Main 981938a draws the scene straight to the canvas (`antialias: false`). Each material either tone-maps itself with three's ACES fit or opts out (`toneMapped: false`) and writes display colours. The land, walkers, wakes, ship foam and lamps, stars, rain, spray, the whale's splash and two underwater layers opt out, so they sit outside the curve the sky, water, whale and ships go through. The water compensated with a reflection gain of 0.8 / exposure for land seen in its mirror. Nothing could be brighter than white.

## Decisions

### three's own HDR output
three r186 has an output path in `WebGLRenderer`: `outputBufferType: HalfFloatType` renders the scene into a half-float target (multisampled 4x when `antialias` is set), runs the effects passed to `setEffects`, then applies the renderer's tone mapping and colour space in one full-screen pass. That is exactly the pipeline the finish needs, so `src/finish.ts` adds only the effects: three's `UnrealBloomPass` and one grade `ShaderPass`. No `EffectComposer`, no hand-written resolve or output pass. The output pass maps every pixel of the target, cleared ones included, so under water, where the clear colour is the water everything fades to, the finish clears to its scene light (`clearInScene`); with the display colour it went through the curve twice and showed as a dark navy band behind the seabed's skyline.

Support is probed once on a throwaway WebGL 2 context, and the frame takes one of three modes (`FrameMode`): `finish` with `EXT_color_buffer_float` or `EXT_color_buffer_half_float` and at least 4 samples for `RGBA16F` on a GPU; `multisampled` on any other GPU whose canvas multisamples, which draws straight to an antialiased canvas without the bloom and grade; `plain` on a CPU renderer (SwiftShader, llvmpipe), as on main. On a CPU renderer the finish took the desktop frame from a median of 417 to 1,167 ms (software GL, seed 42, this machine). `?debug=1&frame=` asks for a mode wherever the browser can draw it, for review shots and the browser smoke. The scene looks the same in every mode apart from the bloom, grade and antialiasing, because of the next decision.

### One curve, exact inverse for display colours
Every material draws scene radiance. A material authored in display colours ends its fragment shader with `sceneFromDisplay`, the closed-form inverse of three's fit (`src/tone.ts`): the output matrix inverted, the rational fit solved as its positive quadratic root, the input matrix inverted, divided by the exposure. On the HDR path the output applies the curve and the colour comes back as authored; on the direct path `#include <tonemapping_fragment>` does the same per material. The CPU form (`radianceFor`) replaces the bisection the underwater view used: identical on its palette, and exact where the bisection's clamp was 2 percent off (the near-white caustic). The fit is held under 0.985 before inverting, so white is a finite radiance (about 18 at noon), and bloom sees it.

Built-in materials convert through `drawAsAuthored`, an `onBeforeCompile` patch before `tonemapping_fragment`; shader materials end with `DRAW_AS_AUTHORED_GLSL`. Rain and spray were written as sRGB values with no colour space step, so they decode with three's `sRGBTransferEOTF` first. `displayExposure` is one shared uniform object written once a frame after the sky sets the exposure.

### Bloom and grade
Bloom starts at a display brightness, `BLOOM_FROM` 0.95, converted to radiance at each frame's exposure, so the sun, its glitter and sunlit spray bloom the same at noon and at dusk. Only the light past the threshold blooms, held under one more threshold's worth (`BLOOM_CEILING`): three's high pass blooms the whole of a pixel's radiance, and the sun is hundreds of times the threshold, which flooded a third of the frame with white in the first shots. Strength 0.32, with the five levels weighted 1, 0.55, 0.3, 0.15, 0.06 (radius 0 keeps them as given): with three's defaults the widest level laid a golden veil over islands under a morning sun. The grade works in scene radiance before the curve: saturation 1.1 (ACES greys bright colours), a split tone (light to 1.05/1.0/0.94, shade to 0.96/0.99/1.05), a vignette of 0.24 from a third of the way out to the corners, and relative interleaved-gradient noise of plus or minus 0.4 percent, about half an 8-bit step after the curve. The grain is fixed in screen space, so it never moves under reduced motion.

### Water
The reflection gain becomes a constant 0.85 (reflected land a touch darker than the land) and the haze it lifts toward is converted to scene light. Glitter is a Blinn term toward the sun at exponent 1400 (single facets, 70 in scene light at a clear noon) plus a sheen at 160 (1.6), scaled by the sun's strength (`sun.intensity / SUN_INTENSITY`, 0 at night and under cloud over the sun) and faded as the sun reaches the horizon. Shallows fade out between 150 and 900 m and vanish at grazing angles (eye elevation under 0.004); they were capped at 25 percent there and drew a band. Storm foam is a crest cap times a ripple lace plus wind streaks, capped at 75 percent, in place of a squared crest that lay in flat sheets.

### Land tiles dissolve by coverage
In the `finish` and `multisampled` modes a tile fading in writes its fade as alpha with `alphaToCoverage`, so it covers that share of each pixel's samples: a true cross-fade over the bare tile or the sea. Only the `plain` mode keeps the screen-space dither, which drew visible cross-hatching on whole islands for the first seconds of a visit.

### Land
Palette moved from lime toward photographed land: grass 0x667a44, meadow 0x848a58, straw 0x9e9268, forest 0x34492c and 0x263a24, rock 0x78726a, cliff 0x6a645c to 0x8e877c, sand 0xd3c4a2. Shade takes the hue of the sky's top haze stop at the same brightness (30 percent, capped at 1.6 per channel), and sunlight takes the sun's colour at the old tint's brightness, so golden hour lights the ranges warm and night shade is moonlit. The sun is a stronger key against the sky's fill (fill 0.36 + 0.16 n.y to 0.3 + 0.14 n.y, sun 0.78 to 0.92), so slopes away from it fall into shade, and cliff banding is halved, both in the vertex colours and in the near strata.

### Land tile grid
Every level had the same cell count, so the angle one cell spans (about 3 degrees at the near edge of its level, 1.5 at the far edge) was the same everywhere, and too big for a skyline: mountains read as cones with straight flanks. Tiles from level 2 (512 m) up now take twice the tier's cells (`tileCells`), halving that angle; the finer sampling also lowers the ring, which adds one octave of ridges and spurs. Level 2 is the first fine level because its spacing (10.7 m at 24 base cells) equals level 1's, so that seam has no step (a test pins it). Shading maps keep their size, so worker time per tile barely moves (Node, high tier: level 3 49 ms against 54, level 4 45 against 61, within noise). A finished tile drops the vertices no triangle uses (only 32 to 46 percent were used), which holds memory, transfer and upload near the old figures. The main thread keeps the tier grid for the first frame and the no-worker fallback, inside its frame budget. Rejected: three times the cells (no visible gain in the side view for 40 percent more triangles) and choosing each quad's diagonal by height (little change).

### Sky at low sun
The sky model scatters once, so away from a low sun its horizon runs brown, then olive, then blue. A JavaScript port of the shader (agreeing with the browser to one sRGB level) searched turbidity, rayleigh, mie, the lobe, exposure, sun height and the glow terms: no setting gave a sky both clear and golden, and a sage band stayed at 2 to 5 degrees. So the glow also drives a warm tint that multiplies the sky and thins upward with the sine of the elevation (`BELT_TINT` 2.8, 0.9, 0.75; `BELT_FALLOFF` 6.4): orange at the horizon, lilac-grey above, then the model's own blue. It follows the glow, so it fades under cloud and as the sun sets, and it leaves the sun disc alone, which sits near the top of what a half float holds (multiplying it drew a black disc). The tint thins below the horizon too: there the sea hides the sky on screen, but the sky stands in for the sea in the reflection environment, and a full tint over that lower half lit the breaching whale's belly a saturated pink and maroon (desktop breach, seed 42: whale pixels about 82, 64, 73 sRGB; now 67, 61, 70, grey with a little sunset warmth). The visible sky is unchanged (identical within 2 levels away from the logo). Phones build their environment once and keep it for the whole visit, through every time of day, so theirs leaves the belt out altogether (a few minutes of the day would otherwise tint the rest, as the diving whale did in mauve), and it dims by the sky's light against the light it was built in, so sails no longer glow at night as they did on main. Golden and dusk take clearer air (turbidity 3, rayleigh 0.55 and 0.6, lobe 0.5; golden exposure 0.54).

Their haze colours are measured with `tools/haze-measure.ts`: the plain frame under reduced motion, water, land, ships and whale hidden, clear weather, each HAZE_ANGLES elevation read straight away from the sun as the median of five azimuths. On the unchanged presets it reproduced morning and afternoon within 5 sRGB levels; golden and dusk differed by up to 38 because they had been measured before the low-sun glow existed, and the night haze (a hand-set navy) was never the measured sky, which is black. The haze no longer takes `warm()` a second time from the glow: the measured colours hold the sunset.

### Whale skin
From the chase camera the back read as a glassy blue capsule. The scene's sky light is strongly blue, so the old blue albedo lit by it turned saturated navy, and the reflection terms (base Fresnel, clear coat, sheen), which do not scale with albedo, were as bright as the skin and mirrored the pale sky at the camera's grazing angles; the clear coat turned a low head-on view into a milky dome. Its `envMapIntensity` of 0.6 never applied: a material without its own `envMap` takes `scene.environmentIntensity` (the sails' 0.08 was dead too and is removed). The skin colours now carry only a trace of blue (back sRGB 0.30, 0.34, 0.37 from 0.25, 0.31, 0.38, the rest alike; the mottle layout and its random streams are unchanged); the physical material drops the clear coat and sets `specularIntensity` 0.3 and a faint grey sheen (0.2, 0x9aa6ae) for the silhouette; the standard material on phones, which cannot weaken its reflection, goes to roughness 0.9. The review hook `setDay` now refreshes the reflection environment where it follows the sky, so review shots at another time of day no longer mirror the opening sky. Under water the lights dimmed and turned blue-green with depth but the environment still lit the whale with the full daylight sky, which the lighter skin showed as a glowing blue whale in dark polar water; the underwater view now fades the sky's share of the environment with depth by the measure that tints the lights (to 0.3 by 40 m, `SKY_IN_DEEP_WATER`), from the level the sky sets (`environmentLevel`), so at 8 m the whale is as before and deep down it is a dim slate shape.

### Storm foam and rough-water reflection
Foam has its own world-anchored texture instead of reading the ripple normal map, which mips to its mean with distance and turned crest masks into smooth sheets. The texture is value noise on a lattice in the wind's frame (period 1024 m, octaves from 32 m to 0.25 m across the wind, streaked downwind and warped). The CPU reduces the floating origin and a downwind drift (half the long roller's speed, from the swell clock) to the period in float64 and passes `foamShift`, so the pattern stays on the world across recentres and stops with the clock under reduced motion. Each octave fades per axis once a pixel spans it and the rest are renormalised to constant contrast, so distance leaves coarse patches, not a sheet; the footprint is capped at 4 m so edge-on swell faces keep patches, and foam fades out by 1000 m. Foam is skipped below sea level 0.3 (a uniform branch), so calm and moderate seas are unchanged (the calm dusk and seed 1234 views are pixel-identical). Rough water tints its mirrored haze by how far each swell face turns toward the eye, pivoted on the usual tilt so the overall brightness holds, and wide gusts vary the reflection of faces seen edge on. `tiledNoise` moved to the shared `GLSL_TILED_NOISE` with a per-axis period. Rejected: foam from the Jacobian of the chop (too weak with 8 waves and a 0.8 chop gain); a separate lane noise for spindrift (read as straight scratches).

### Streaming budget
The browser smoke's streaming check (under 10 percent of 30 Hz cruise steps over 3 ms) sat at its bar on a four-core machine on main, and the finer land grid adds work. Two causes showed in per-part timings. Every visible ship rewrote all of its sail vertices each step outside the budget (up to 7,056 for a tall ship, about 0.2 ms a ship); the cloth moves under a pixel beyond a few hundred metres, so ships now sway their sails only within 400 m of the whale. And the budgeted loops stopped only after the slice that crossed the budget, with the near shelf bake handing its navigation prefetch the whole 2.5 ms instead of the time left. `SliceCost` (`src/slice-cost.ts`) remembers the longest recent slice, forgetting 3 percent a slice, and the prefetch and both bake loops stop when the time left would not hold another one; the reserve is capped at 1 ms so no job starves. What is built, and in what order, is unchanged: tiles are pure functions of their coordinates, so only the timing moves. Rejected: splitting the prefetch into near tiles and a capped lead (measured worse).

## Risks

- Half-float multisampling costs bandwidth on phones. The quality tiers already lower the pixel ratio when frames run long; device figures are owed (`FOLLOWUP.md`).
- Blending of transparent display-authored layers (foam, rain, the waterline) now happens in scene light. That is closer to how light adds, and the layers were checked in shots, but they are not pixel-identical to main.
